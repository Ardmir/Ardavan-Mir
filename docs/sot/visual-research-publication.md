Prepared and approved: 2026-10-03. Status: owner approved the reviewed public content and publication through the existing GitHub Pages workflow.

## Proposed public scope

- `/research/visual-atlas`: screenshot-led collection index using the portfolio's manuscript typography, palette, and editorial rules.
- `builders/index.html`: September 3 external visual anatomy study of 15 products and five interface structures.
- `developer-builders/index.html`: September 3 visual mechanism audit of 60 states across 13 external products, with search, filters, full-size views, and public source registers.
- `mobile/index.html`: August 19 mobile and ambient study; 105 retained frames across 26 product families, including supporting August 18 desktop states and 14 sequence montages.

Gallery paths are beneath `/research/visual-atlas/`. The existing Research navigation leads to the research collection, which prominently links to the visual index. The homepage research entry also leads to the new index.

## Evidence boundaries

External public-product research only. Internal application hypotheses, confidential product material, private source links, chapter/story mappings, and source-machine paths are excluded from these assets. Original evidence images are unchanged.

One mobile frame with unknown original provenance is excluded. Mobile evidence comprises 76 App Store marketing frames, 20 adjacent interaction analogs, seven official-documentation frames, and two official-demo frames. Most mobile evidence is promotional material rather than direct signed-in use. Seven desktop examples link to explicitly labeled related mobile sequences.

Developer source URLs are product-level source sets, rather than claimed exact screenshot-to-page mappings. The Copilot publication-error crop is described only as visible open agent issues; a data-policy interpretation is not asserted from that image.

Snapshots retain their original observation dates. They do not establish current product availability or technical architecture. Public vendor and visual-source attribution remains attached to each study.
